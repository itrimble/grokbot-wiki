# The switch and the 100 Mb/s NAS

On 2026-11-03 I plugged a new 2.5G switch into the network and lost the NAS. It took most of an afternoon to get a stable 2.5G link back, and I never found the root cause. Here is what I saw, in order.

## What happened

1. **The NAS vanished from the network** when it went on the new switch. The wired Macs behind the switch dropped off too. The switch ships with its Loop Prevention slider off.
2. **I turned Loop Prevention on and replugged the cables.** The wired machines came back. I did not isolate which of the two steps fixed it.
3. **The NAS went on the switch a second time, on a different cable, and dropped again.**
4. **Back on the Deco, the NAS linked at 100 Mb/s.** Its own status page, the kernel's link counters and a throughput test all agreed: 11.6 MB/s. Two different Deco ports and two Cat6A cables made no difference, and the NAS has no link-speed setting to change.
5. **A full shutdown with the power cable out for about 30 seconds fixed it.** The NAS came back at 2500 Mb/s. An ordinary restart earlier had not.
6. **On the switch afterward,** the NAS held 2500 Mb/s with no errors, and 0 of 60 pings were lost to four hosts at once. Pulling data to a gigabit laptop ran at 107 to 110 MB/s, which is that laptop's limit.

## What I do not know

I do not know why the link got stuck at 100 Mb/s. The NAS network chip is an Intel I226-V. In 2023, Intel staff acknowledged random connection drops on that chip and suggested turning off Energy Efficient Ethernet as a mitigation ([Intel Community thread](https://community.intel.com/t/intel-communication-intel-ethernet-controller-i226-series-random-connection-drops-at-random-times/111801)). That is about drops, not about falling back to 100 Mb/s, so it only partly fits. I have not confirmed it as the cause here.

I also cannot say whether Loop Prevention or the replug fixed the first failure, and I checked the new link for only a short time after boot. I will recheck the link speed and the link's change counter after a few days.

## What I would tell you

- **Read the negotiated speed after every cable or switch change.** A link can work and still be ten times too slow, and nothing warns you.
- **Try a full power cycle before you blame the cable.** I swapped cables and ports first and lost time. The 30-second power-off was the only thing that worked.
- **Turn on loop prevention** if your unmanaged switch has it. It costs nothing.
- **Measure with something faster than your laptop.** A gigabit port hides a 2.5G fix.
- **Write down the switch ports.** I did not, and on an unmanaged switch the only way to find out later is to trace the cables.
